The Mentalist - Mobile Application

Perform mind-reading tricks, psychological illusions, and stunning predictions—all from your phone.

Dart Flutter

Overview

The Mentalist turns a phone into a secret prop for a classic "any card you name" mentalism routine. On the surface it just shows a playing card that flips over on tap — but underneath is a performer-only control panel: the magician can silently force any rank and suit onto the reveal, or disguise the phone entirely as an idle lock screen (fake clock, fake battery percentage, fake airplane-mode/Wi-Fi-off icons) so a spectator glancing at it sees nothing suspicious.

It's a single-screen Flutter app built around one StatefulWidget (MagicCardScreen) and a custom CardWidget that renders a 3D card-flip using raw Matrix4 transforms rather than an animation package. A normal picker lets the magician openly set the card and its back design before a trick. A second, hidden control — reachable only via a long-press gesture — unlocks "Secret Mode", where taps on specific regions of the card's own back silently redefine what the "reveal" shows next.

The core design problem was making the secret input channel invisible without making it unusable in practice: a spectator must never see a menu or a tell, but the magician needs to reliably hit one of fifteen invisible zones on a card they're holding, under performance pressure. The app solves this with a debug overlay (also long-press-gated) that draws the invisible grid and highlights the last tapped cell, letting the magician rehearse the exact hand position for each rank until it's muscle memory.

Key features

  • 3D card-flip reveal: a Transform with a manually-set perspective entry (setEntry(3, 2, 0.001)) rotates the card 0–π on the Y axis over 2.5 seconds, swapping from the back face to a counter-rotated front face at the halfway point so the artwork never appears mirrored.
  • Open card picker: a bottom-sheet CardPicker lets the magician choose any of the 13 ranks (including both jokers) and 4 suits as chips, plus a CardBackPicker to swap between four card-back designs — used for the honest, "no trick here" setup phase.
  • Secret Mode: a long press on the "Imposta Carta" button silently arms a hidden input mode with no visible UI change, so the spectator has no reason to suspect anything just happened.
  • Invisible 3×5 rank grid on the card back: once Secret Mode is armed, tapping one of fifteen regions on the card's own back (mapped in a row,col → rank table covering A through K plus both jokers) sets the rank that will be revealed — the magician "reads" the card by touch alone while appearing to just be holding it.
  • Clock-driven suit selection: in Secret Mode the suit is derived from the unit digit of the device's current second at the moment of the tap (1–2→hearts, 3–4→diamonds, 5–6→clubs, 7–8→spades), turning the visible on-screen clock into a second, independent secret channel timed to the performer's own tap.
  • Debug grid overlay: a separate long press on the fake battery icon toggles a CustomPainter overlay that draws the 3×5 grid lines and highlights the last tapped cell in green — purely a rehearsal aid, meant to be off during an actual performance.
  • Disguised status bar: a fully custom top bar fakes a phone's real status indicators — a live HH:mm:ss clock, airplane-mode and Wi-Fi-off icons, and a battery percentage that even ticks from 68% to 86% once Secret Mode is armed, reinforcing the illusion that the phone is just sitting idle.
  • Animated splash screen: a 2.5s intro plays a circular progress sweep, scale/opacity tweens, and a color transition into the app's blue-grey theme before handing off to the main screen with a cross-fade.
  • Immersive, orientation-locked full screen: the app forces portrait-only orientation and immersive-sticky system UI (with a platform channel to detect Android's gesture vs. button navigation mode) so no OS chrome breaks the disguise.
  • Responsive card sizing: a LayoutBuilder computes the largest card size that fits the available space while preserving the standard 5:7 playing-card aspect ratio, so the layout holds up across phone sizes.

Tech stack

Dart

Implements the entire trick logic: the row/column-to-rank lookup table for the secret grid, the seconds-unit-digit-to-suit mapping, the tap-region math that turns a raw TapDownDetails position into a grid cell, and the state machine coordinating the picker, Secret Mode, and Debug Mode without ever leaking a visible cue.

Flutter

Drives the 3D flip with hand-built Matrix4/Transform perspective rather than a package, paints the debug grid and tapped-cell highlight with a custom CustomPainter, and uses SystemChrome plus a MethodChannel to lock orientation and immersive full-screen mode across Android's different navigation styles.
